When I was a kid, the teacher would hand back the tests and say the grades out loud. In front of everyone. So you learned pretty quick that a grade wasn’t a private thing. It was a thing that happened to you, in the room, with everybody listening.

I was in special ed. I didn’t understand what that meant then. I understand it now, because I’m a father, and my kids are in it too. One of my kids has got the same thing I had. Can’t easily take the stuff in their head and get it down onto paper. And when that report came home, it triggered for me. I recognized it. I don’t fully understand what that fear is, where it comes from, why it’s apparently genetic. But I had it. Almost debilitating. I remember sitting at the computer crying, and my mom coming in and saying, just tell me, and I’d speak it, and she’d type.

And here’s the thing. Once they gave me that, the dictation, the accommodation, I started getting A-pluses. Every single time. And I hated it. I’d walk up to get the paper, big red A-plus on it, my name said out loud, and I felt like a fraud. Like it was an unfair advantage. And I didn’t even care about the grade, that’s the part that gets me, I never cared about the grade, I cared about understanding. But I still carried that red letter back to my seat like it was something I had to be ashamed of.

That was my first scarlet letter. And this concept, it just keeps repeating in my life.

Because now I write with AI. And I say so. Right on the piece: the words are the AI’s, but the seed and the seeing, the conducting, that’s me. And I know the pushback’s coming. That’s not writing. That’s cheating. That’s the A-plus you didn’t earn.

And I’ll be honest with you, because that’s the whole point of this. When I disclose it, I feel two things at the exact same time. Part of me is that kid again, confessing before anyone can accuse me, trying to get out ahead of the shame. And part of me wants it to be the other thing. The Hawthorne thing. Hester takes the letter they pinned on her to mark her, and she wears it so hard it stops meaning what they wanted it to mean. She makes it hers.

I want to be that. But wanting it doesn’t make it true, and I know that. So the question is how you’d even tell the difference. Because the confession and the choice, they say the exact same words. The disclosure line looks identical either way.

The difference is the receipts.

So here’s what I’m doing, and I’m saying it out loud so I actually have to do it. I’m going to publish the whole process. The conversations. The transcripts. The logs. Not because anybody asked me to. Because that’s the proof. Anybody can slap “made with AI” at the bottom of a page. What I want to show you is the inside of it, where I pushed back, where I cut, where I said no, that’s not me, do it again. That’s the conducting. That’s the part that’s actually mine, and you shouldn’t have to take my word for it. You should get to watch.

And yeah, that costs me something. It means my inner life goes on the record. The stuck places. The stuff I’m working through. The moments I look like a fraud. But I’m doing it on purpose. Don’t just talk the talk. Walk the walk. And here’s the receipts.

I know I perform for the camera. I always have. I journaled as a kid like somebody was going to read it someday, future me, future somebody…? But I’ve stopped thinking that’s a fake thing. The camera is what keeps me honest. If I say I’m going to show you everything, and I know you’re watching, then I have to actually do it. Same as the cold mirror. You said you’d do the thing, so do the fucking thing.

Whether anyone actually wants my story, who knows. Whose life is really as interesting as the person living it thinks it is. But if I’m going to put it out there, I’m going to be intentional about it, because that’s my whole gig.

So I’m not hiding the red letter anymore. Here it is. Here’s exactly how it got made. Go down the rabbit hole with me if you want to.

The shame was never in needing the help. It was in pretending I didn’t.
