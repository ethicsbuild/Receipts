# What They Are Afraid Of: watch items

Things that happened after this piece was published and that bear on what it claims.

This file is not anchored on Hedera, because it changes. The record and the page it belongs to are anchored and do not change. Where something here turns out to correct the essay or the record, the correction is made in those files and said out loud, and this note says where.

Each entry separates what is established from what is only suspected, because that separation is the whole argument of the piece and it would be cheap to abandon it the first time a headline flattered the thesis.

## 2026-10-29: refrigeration failures at military commissaries, treated as a suspected cyberattack

**What is established.** In late August 2026, refrigeration at on-base commissaries, which are the grocery stores on US military installations, went into defrost mode overnight and spoiled the food. Reuters counted at least six affected, from the bases' own Facebook posts and websites. The Fort Huachuca account wrote on August 26 that its freezer "went into defrost mode where it heated up all the food." The Pentagon has acknowledged the refrigeration failures and says a Cyber Command unit is "working in close coordination with interagency and law enforcement partners." The Army's Criminal Investigation Division says its Cyber Field Office is involved and is working with French law enforcement on the refrigeration outages in Europe. In mid-October, more than 350 food businesses in France had refrigeration settings altered overnight, and some units overheated badly. In August, the security firm Claroty published vulnerabilities in Danfoss and Copeland refrigeration controllers, including a hidden login that allows full control. Danfoss has released a fix, and told Reuters that many of the affected French units were old or running outdated software.

**What is only suspected.** That any of this was a cyberattack at all. The claim rests on four officials who are not named. Reuters states that it could not determine how far the investigation has progressed, whether it has reached any conclusion, or who might be responsible. No country is named. No intrusion into any control system at a US base has been confirmed. Reuters could not establish that the affected American stores even used the controller brand that was found to be vulnerable.

**What it does not change in the essay.** The essay says that no outage in this country has been publicly blamed on China. That is still true, because nothing here is attributed to anyone. The essay also says that what the agencies documented about Volt Typhoon is access, rather than that access being used to break something. This is a separate matter, tied to no actor at all, and it concerns the freezers in base grocery stores rather than the power grid. Nothing in the published piece needs correcting on account of this story.

**What it would change if it were confirmed.** It would be the first case I am aware of in which equipment on American military installations physically failed and officials publicly treated the cause as an intrusion. It would also be the clearest illustration of the finding this essay's research reached and the essay itself did not use: in ordinary commodity hardware the realistic risk is switches, not bombs. Nothing exploded. A setting changed, and the food went bad. And it would repeat the structure the essay spends its longest section on, which is that the fix reached the vendor while the deployed thing kept running the old software.

**Status: suspected, not established.** By the test this essay sets for everyone else, it cannot be called a cyberattack until someone willing to put a name to it says so.
