## 2026-11-12: the essay taken down and republished, with the manifesto's receipts restored

**What happened.** The morning it ran, Gage said what he had liked about his original manifesto was "the data, the facts," and that putting them back mattered "because that was the whole thesis originally." He deleted the post, and the essay was republished the same morning at the same address.

**What changed in the essay.**

- The one paragraph summarizing the manifesto's receipts became three. They restore the manifesto's own evidence, rechecked against sources: the third-party doctrine and Carpenter v. United States; Latanya Sweeney's finding that most Americans can be identified from zip code, birthday and sex; the AOL search release and the New York Times finding a widow in Georgia from her searches; the Netflix Prize ratings matched against public reviews; Clearview AI's scraped photo database, "more than thirty billion photos, by its own CEO's count"; Target's pregnancy predictions from shopping; and early research on typing rhythm and depression.
- Left out of the manifesto's list: "your political affiliation from your podcast app" (no source found) and the famous angry-father detail of the Target story (disputed). The typing line is hedged as "early research suggests," because the study behind it has 24 people.
- Grammar fixes: "the sci-fi movie feelings, all of them at once"; a double space removed; a missing period after "Remember that distinction"; "at fifteen or in your worst moments"; the village "became an obstacle to change" (it said "made it an obstacle").
- One wording change, the machine's, for clarity: "These visionaries" became "The visionaries behind these cameras."
- Two new section breaks: before "Here's what's new," and before "So fake it till you make it works."

**Snowden.** Gage remembered the manifesto's receipts as including "the Snowden stuff." The manifesto does not mention Snowden, and the machine said so. He is not in the essay.
