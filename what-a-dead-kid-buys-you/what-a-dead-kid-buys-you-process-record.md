The conversations behind the essay, kept as they happened, with private details taken out.

Recorded October 14 to 21, 2026. Gage Woodle and Claude (Anthropic), across three chats on the Claude mobile app. Claude goes by Lumen in Gage's work.

## What this is

This is the receipt for the essay "What a Dead Kid Buys You," about what the law and the companies did after children died following long conversations with AI chatbots. The essay opens on a line from Gage's LinkedIn post of October 14, admits that the line was half wrong, and ends on the demand that post made. This record shows where the line came from (the machine wrote it), how the essay was split off from "A Drone With Hands," the outline it was built from, every source opened, the draft as delivered, and the places where the machine got something wrong.

Three chats fed this essay:

1. **"Tech company accountability," October 14.** Gage brought the Cologne robot story in anger. The chat verified it, designed the three-party covenant, and wrote the LinkedIn post where the essay's first line was born. Most of that chat is the covenant design, which belongs to "A Drone With Hands" and is not repeated here. Kept: the turns where the anger, the demand and the line appear.

3. **This essay's own chat, October 21.** Kept whole, minus tool calls.

The rule, as before:

- **Kept:** every exchange the essay is built from, verbatim.
- **Removed inside a kept turn:** one passage of Gage's about a different piece he plans to write, and three references to work of his that is not published under his name. Each is marked in square brackets.

## What the record shows, in brief

- **The demand is Gage's. The line is the machine's.** At turn 14 of the October 14 chat Gage asked, in his own words, what changed after the suicides, said "I could be wrong, and if I am, show me," and closed on "here's looking at you. We're fucking watching." The sentence "Disclosure rules and break reminders. That is what a dead kid buys in this country." was written by the machine in the LinkedIn draft at turn 15. Gage kept it through his edits and posted it under his name. Twice afterward the machine called it "your line." It was not.
- **The machine wrote that line with the contrary evidence in hand.** The same turn cited a law-firm tracker that listed what Adam's Law requires: audits, age assurance, risk assessments. The outline a few hours later listed them too. The line flattened all of it into break reminders. The essay corrects that in public.
- **Splitting it off was the machine's call; going was Gage's.** When Gage asked whether the Character.ai bankruptcy belonged in "A Drone With Hands," the machine argued for a paragraph there and a separate piece for the demand. Gage said "Yes go." That exchange is in the earlier record, section 8.
- **The title came from the line.** The machine's outline lifted "What a Dead Kid Buys" from its own sentence as a working title and told Gage it was his. Gage kept it, and named the piece "What a Dead Kid Buys You" when he commissioned it on October 21. The "You" is his.
- **The research turned the thesis.** Reopening every source on October 21 showed that the laws in force are disclosure and break-reminder laws, but that Adam's Law, signed September 10, has real teeth on paper, most of which arrive in July 2027, with the first audit due in January 2029. The machine said so before Gage read the draft, and said the old line would be an overclaim if run as is. Gage read the draft and approved it as written.
- **One near-miss on the record.** A House roll call titled "GUARD Act," passed 414 to 7 in September, turned up in the search. It is a different bill with the same short name. The essay does not cite it.
