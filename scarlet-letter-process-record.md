# The Scarlet Letter: Process Record

The conversation that produced the essay, kept as it happened.

Recorded September 25, 2026. Gage Woodle and Claude (Anthropic), inside the AI for All project. Most of it was in voice mode.

## What this is

This is the receipt for the essay "The Scarlet Letter." The essay says that anyone can write "made with AI" at the bottom of a page, and that the proof of authorship is in the process: where I pushed back, where I cut, where I said no. This file is that process.

## How to read it

- Gage's lines are his speech as the voice transcription caught it. They are not cleaned up. The false starts and repeated words are his.
- Claude's lines are verbatim.
- Nothing has been removed. One detail has been changed: which of Gage's kids has the accommodation. Every place that said so, his words and Claude's, now says "child" or "kid," in square brackets where it alters a spoken line. That is his child's information, not his, and he chose to protect it.
- A few notes are marked [Record note]. They sit outside the conversation and say what happened that the transcript alone would not show.

## What the record shows, in brief

- The analogy at the center, working with AI as the adult form of a school dictation accommodation, came from Gage in his first message. Claude named where the analogy breaks: a scribe transcribes, and Claude composes.
- The scarlet letter came from Gage. Claude cut him off mid-thought, and he made Claude stop and let him finish. The story he finished, the A-plus read aloud and carried back to his seat in shame, became the spine of the essay.
- The Hawthorne connection was Claude's. The question it raised, whether disclosure is choosing the mark or confessing to escape it, was answered by Gage: both, at once.
- The idea of publishing transcripts with the book was Gage's, from an earlier conversation. Performance as commitment, the cold mirror, was Gage's. "Sacrifice" was Gage's word.
- The decision to publish under his own name and not as Roan Wilder was Claude's recommendation, and Gage accepted it.
- Claude's first draft was in Claude's own literary cadence. Gage rejected it: "don't follow your literary formats." The second draft was rebuilt from his spoken lines. Some of those lines are still Claude's paraphrase, not his words. The list is at the end.
